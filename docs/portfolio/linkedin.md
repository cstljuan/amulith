# LinkedIn draft

Amulith started with a question: what if the thing still awake inside a cursed gem was a real, local AI?

At Knight Hacks IX, Lawrence Capistrano and I built a forest game around a borrowed NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano. The original prototype connected a local Qwen model, a shrine camera, an Arduino relic, and a Three.js world with two opposing paths: Bloom and Blight.

I spent a substantial part of the weekend getting the Jetson running and making inference coexist with rendering on the same GPU. I also directed the game's art, visual identity, interaction design, and world-building. Lawrence built and integrated the gameplay systems and level progression.

After returning the Jetson, I wanted the project to remain something people could actually experience. I've been developing a portable browser edition with an authored reactive script, branching conversations, faction endings, and a new identity in Figma.

What interests me most is the connection between AI, physical interaction, and storytelling: making a system feel like something you can approach, question, and respond to.

Built at Knight Hacks IX, and continuing beyond the weekend.
