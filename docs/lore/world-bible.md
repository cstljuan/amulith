# World bible

## Established Knight Hacks lore, as recorded in DECISIONS.md

At the end of Knight Hacks VIII, the gem shattered and Lenny was freed from its control. The pieces sank into soil. Roots wrapped them, and a forest grew too fast. The amulet is six shards in different biomes; its curse has made half the forest toxic.

T.K., the knight, went missing in the forest. He lies moss-covered beside Lenny and will not wake. Something left behind by the gem is still awake deeper in the enchanted forest. The locked spec identifies that something as Amulith for this game.

Bloom comprises Whisperseed, Dryad and Petalborn. Its aim is to destroy the shards and free T.K. and Lenny. Blight comprises Chomper, Spora and Collosus. Its aim is to gather the shards and keep the forest corrupted. Preserve the spelling Collosus used by the spec.

These facts establish the starting situation. They do not supply a date for the forest's growth, the knight's private thoughts, an explanation for his sleep or a history for individual faction members. Do not fill those gaps with claims of official canon.

## Fan-project interpretation

Amulith remembers being broken. It calls the roots its body and the separated pieces parts of itself. It wants wholeness, and treats the forest's corruption as a form of belonging. These are the project's interpretation of the presence left behind by the gem, not additional official Knight Hacks history.

The Wanderer is the player character in this game. The lantern makes hidden things visible while inviting danger. The stone-circle shrine is a place to address Amulith; its quiet invitation makes the choice feel personal before the journey grows hostile. The physical Lenny figures select a side in the game. A player's choice does not establish Lenny's beliefs or reverse the canonical fact that he was freed from the gem's control.

This game resolves three selected shard sites from a world containing six shards. Do not describe that run target as the total number of shards in the amulet. Bloom's game ending has the last required shard break and T.K. stand; Blight's has the pieces fuse and the treeline close. These endings are this fan project's outcomes, not official sequels to Knight Hacks IX.

## History since the fracture

1. The gem shatters at Knight Hacks VIII. Lenny is freed.
2. Pieces settle into the soil. Roots enfold them, and the forest grows too quickly.
3. The curse poisons half the forest. T.K. is found asleep under moss beside Lenny.
4. In Amulith's interpretation, a remaining consciousness speaks through the roots. Bloom seeks release through destruction; Blight seeks continued corruption through reunion.
5. The Wanderer's run begins at the shrine. The ending depends on the player's allegiance and actions.

The first three steps rest on the locked spec. The final two describe this game's framing. No calendar, kingdom, past war, named ancestor or unseen betrayal is established here.

## Places, proposed for this game

### Shrine Grove (`grove`)

A stone circle anchors the existing hero area. Warm lantern light catches the edges of the shrine while roots press close enough to make its welcome ambiguous. This is where the Wanderer first learns that the forest can answer.

Bloom reads the circle as a threshold toward freeing the sleepers. Blight reads it as a place to promise the gem its scattered pieces. Neither reading makes the shrine an official Knight Hacks temple with an invented founder or religious order.

Writing cues: stone warmed by a lantern, moss against the sleeping knight, roots listening at the circle. Amulith begins gently here: "Come closer. My roots have room for you."

### Mushroom Hollow (`hollow`)

Caps crowd the path and catch small sounds beneath them. The second progression level should feel enclosed, with the shard's quiet hum drawing attention toward the soil rather than the horizon. This is a proposed writing atmosphere, not a claim that a new map or sound effect exists.

Bloom sees growth forced to flourish on a curse. Blight sees shelter and abundance worth preserving. Neither side needs a speech about being good or evil; its attitude emerges in what it protects.

Writing cues: crowded caps, spores in lantern light, footsteps muffled under growth. Amulith offers closeness as comfort: "Walk softly. Even broken things need shelter."

### Rootdeep (`deepwood`)

The last progression level moves deeper into the forest. Root crossings feel like knuckles curled around something precious. The remaining pieces make Amulith's wish to be whole harder to disguise.

Bloom approaches the roots as a hold that must be loosened. Blight approaches them as a home to complete. Reserve the strongest accusations and invitations for this depth without inventing a hidden monarch, new curse or final boss.

Writing cues: old roots, a hum felt through soil, space closing around the lantern. Amulith's claim is direct: "Those roots are mine. So is what they shelter."

### Petal Glade, existing wire-zone name

Petal Glade remains the display name for wire zone `glade` in the existing game. It is not a fourth progression level or a replacement name for Shrine Grove. Writing can use petals and golden light where the existing location supports them; map ownership stays with the level session.

## Factions and beliefs

The goals and class names below are established in the locked lore. Their beliefs are proposed fan-project writing directions, not official class biographies or abilities.

| Side | Proposed belief | How its dialogue sounds |
|---|---|---|
| Bloom | Release can require breaking what holds someone captive. Growth should leave room for the sleepers to wake. | Tender toward T.K. and Lenny, wary of the roots' promises, willing to act despite grief |
| Blight | What was scattered should be gathered; the forest's strange life deserves continuity. Amulith calls that continuity belonging. | Protective of the pieces, confident in the forest's claim, drawn to Amulith's approval |

Use Whisperseed, Dryad, Petalborn, Chomper, Spora and Collosus as class flavor. A bark may invoke a class's name without implying the Wanderer belongs to it. Do not assign a mandatory rank, family tree, signature power or named leader. Bloom enemies in a Blight run are Petalborn; Blight enemies in a Bloom run are Chomper, as the current game specifies.

## Characters

**T.K.** is the knight: armor, helmet, sword and energy. He is missing, moss-covered beside Lenny, and will not wake. Describe his visible condition and the stakes of waking him. Do not give him a remembered speech, personal quest or explanation for the curse. His standing in Bloom's ending belongs to this game's outcome.

**Lenny** was controlled by the gem and freed when it shattered. He lies beside the sleeping T.K. The sleepy and angry figures are game selection cues, with the angry figure wearing a petal hat. They are not evidence of a canonical allegiance, secret betrayal or renewed possession. Let his presence make the stakes concrete without narrating thoughts the player cannot know.

**Amulith / the Remnant** is this game's name and voice for the thing left awake. It speaks as a wounded, possessive presence, never as a neutral guide. It tempts Bloom and approves Blight. Its view is interested and unreliable; a claim that the knight is happier asleep is manipulation, not authorial truth. See [the voice guide](remnant-voice.md).

**The Wanderer** is this fan project's playable traveler. Leave their origin open. The player chooses a side and carries the lantern; new ancestry or prophecy is unnecessary.

Any wandering spirits or NPCs added in the content modules are original fan-project figures. They should offer small observations about their surroundings, not certify invented official history.
